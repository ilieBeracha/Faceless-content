### c1 · high confidence · beat `explode`
> Inside a ballpoint pen's plastic barrel is a thin tube (the refill) that holds the ink.
- **Source:** https://home.howstuffworks.com/pen3.htm

### c2 · high confidence · beat `explode`
> Ballpoint pen ink is thick and oil-based — far more viscous than water or fountain-pen ink (oil-based ballpoint inks are typically around 1,000–20,000 centipoise; water is about 1).
- **Source:** https://home.howstuffworks.com/pen3.htm ; US patent 5,990,201 (image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5990201)
- **Note:** Some modern 'low-viscosity' oil inks are thinner (300–3,000 cP) but still far thicker than water.

### c3 · high confidence · beat `explode`
> The tip of a common ballpoint pen holds a metal ball about one millimetre wide (a 'medium' point is a 1.0 mm ball).
- **Source:** MoMA collection, Bic Cristal (moma.org/collection/works/82141): '1 mm ball'; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bic_Cristal
- **Note:** Ball sizes range from about 0.5 to 1.6 mm; materials include tungsten carbide, steel and brass.

### c4 · high confidence · beat `key-part`
> The ball sits in a socket at the tip; as it rolls, ink from the refill coats the ball and the ball carries that ink onto the paper.
- **Source:** https://home.howstuffworks.com/pen3.htm ; Princeton fluid-mechanics paper (princeton.edu/~stonelab/Teaching/PaulReverdy%20finalPaper.pdf)

### c5 · high confidence · beat `key-part`
> When the pen isn't writing, the ball blocks the opening at the tip, which also limits air reaching the ink so it doesn't dry out.
- **Source:** https://home.howstuffworks.com/pen3.htm
- **Note:** 'Like a tiny cork' is an analogy, not a literal seal.

### c6 · high confidence · beat `payoff`
> Ballpoint ink is too viscous to flow out freely on its own; it flows when the rolling ball draws it out.
- **Source:** Bureau of Indian Standards, Ballpoint Pen document (bis.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Ballpoint-Pen-16.pdf): 'the ink does not flow out of the pen freely due to its viscous nature'
