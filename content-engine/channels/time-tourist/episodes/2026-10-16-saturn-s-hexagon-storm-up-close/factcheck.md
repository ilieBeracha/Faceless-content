> Saturn has a long-lasting six-sided (hexagonal) cloud pattern around its north pole.
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn%27s_hexagon ; NASA/JPL, 'Saturn's Mysterious Hexagon Emerges From Winter Darkness' (2009), https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/saturns-mysterious-hexagon-emerges-from-winter-darkness/
- **Note:** Also the on-screen hook text 'Saturn's hexagon'. The hexagon sits at about 78° N.

### c2 · high confidence · beat `arrival`
> Saturn is more than a billion kilometres from Earth.
- **Source:** NASA Saturn facts (about 1.4 billion km from the Sun), https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/facts/ ; ESA Facts about Saturn
- **Note:** Earth–Saturn distance varies from roughly 1.2 to 1.7 billion km; 'over a billion' is true at all times.

### c3 · high confidence · beat `arrival`
> Saturn is a gas giant with no solid surface to stand on.
- **Source:** NASA Solar System Exploration, Saturn: 'As a gas giant, Saturn doesn't have a true surface', https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/facts/ ; ESA Facts about Saturn
- **Note:** It may have a solid core deep inside, but nothing a visitor could land on.

### c4 · high confidence · beat `tour`
> Each side of the hexagon is longer than Earth's diameter (about 13,800–14,500 km per side vs Earth's 12,742 km).
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn%27s_hexagon (sides about 14,500 km) ; Astronomy magazine, 'Weird object: Hexagon on Saturn' (2015), https://www.astronomy.com/science/weird-object-hexagon-on-saturn/ (13,800 km)
- **Note:** Sources give 13,800 or 14,500 km per side; both exceed Earth's diameter, so the line says only 'wider than Earth'.

### c5 · high confidence · beat `tour`
> The jet stream along the hexagon blows at roughly 100 metres per second, over 300 km/h.
- **Source:** NASA/JPL (2009) hexagon release: jet stream at about 100 m/s (220 mph), https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/saturns-mysterious-hexagon-emerges-from-winter-darkness/ ; Space.com, https://www.space.com/7651-saturn-weird-hexagon-images.html
- **Note:** 100 m/s = 360 km/h, 220 mph = 354 km/h; said conservatively as 'over three hundred kilometres an hour'.

### c6 · high confidence · beat `tour`
> The hexagon was discovered in Voyager images in the early 1980s, and NASA's Cassini spacecraft imaged it again decades later.
- **Source:** NASA/JPL (2009): 'originally discovered in images taken by the Voyager spacecraft in the early 1980s' and Cassini's matching images, https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/saturns-mysterious-hexagon-emerges-from-winter-darkness/
- **Note:** Voyager flybys 1980–81; Cassini observed it 2006–2017.

### c7 · high confidence · beat `catch`
> Scientists are not fully sure why the hexagon has six sides; spinning-water lab experiments produce similar polygon shapes.
- **Source:** Barbosa Aguiar, Read et al., 'A laboratory model of Saturn's North Polar Hexagon', Icarus 206 (2010); Smithsonian Magazine, https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/saturns-polar-hexagon-26686194/ ; Rostami et al., Icarus 297 (2017): 'not enough convincing dynamical explanation', https://web.lmd.jussieu.fr/~aspiga/pub/REF/2017Icar..297...59R.pdf
- **Note:** A meandering jet stream is the leading explanation, but it is debated; said as 'nobody's totally sure'.

### c8 · high confidence · beat `catch`
> Saturn's south pole has no matching hexagon.
- **Source:** Rostami et al., Icarus 297 (2017) on 'the absence of its counterpart at Saturn's South pole', https://web.lmd.jussieu.fr/~aspiga/pub/REF/2017Icar..297...59R.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn%27s_hexagon
- **Note:** The south pole has a large polar vortex (hurricane-like storm), but no hexagon.
