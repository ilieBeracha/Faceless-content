## Claims (11)

### c1 · high confidence · beat `hook`
> Perytons at the Parkes radio telescope were caused by on-site microwave ovens, and they clustered around lunchtime (hook: 'Lunch fooled a telescope'; also the title bar).
- **Source:** Petroff et al. 2015, 'Identifying the source of perytons at the Parkes radio telescope', MNRAS 451, 3933; arXiv:1504.02165 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02165), section on relevance to FRBs: 'the clustering around the lunchtime hour'
- **Note:** 'Fooled' = the signals mimicked an astrophysical pulse closely enough to cast doubt on the first FRB.

### c2 · high confidence · beat `clue`
> Perytons have been detected at Parkes since 1998 (46 of them by 2015, 16 on 23 June 1998).
- **Source:** Petroff et al. 2015, arXiv:1504.02165, section 'The Peryton Cluster of 23rd June 1998': 'Of the 46 perytons detected at Parkes since 1998…'
- **Note:** The '1998' sign repeats this year.

### c3 · high confidence · beat `clue`
> Parkes is a radio dish (radio telescope) in Australia.
- **Source:** Petroff et al. 2015, arXiv:1504.02165 (Parkes radio telescope, with the Australia Telescope Compact Array 400 km north of Parkes); Swinburne University news, May 2015 ('The Dish')

### c4 · high confidence · beat `clue`
> Perytons are millisecond-long bursts whose frequency sweep mimics an astrophysical pulse, and their similarity to the first fast radio burst (FRB 010724) cast doubt on FRBs being from deep space.
- **Source:** Petroff et al. 2015, arXiv:1504.02165, abstract: 'their similarity to FRB 010724 had cast doubt on the interpretation of FRBs as extragalactic'

### c5 · high confidence · beat `clue`
> Perytons occurred during office hours, clustered around lunchtime.
- **Source:** Petroff et al. 2015, arXiv:1504.02165 (time-of-day distribution, Figure 7: clustered in office hours, 'particularly around lunch time'); Swinburne University, 'How we found the source of the mystery signals at The Dish' (May 2015), https://www.swinburne.edu.au/news/2015/05/how-we-found-the-source-of-the-mystery-signals-at-the-dish

### c6 · high confidence · beat `story`
> Emily Petroff was a PhD student (Swinburne University of Technology) working on fast radio bursts, and led the peryton study.
- **Source:** Swinburne University news, May 2015 (byline: 'PhD candidate in Astro Physics at Swinburne University of Technology'), https://www.swinburne.edu.au/news/2015/05/how-we-found-the-source-of-the-mystery-signals-at-the-dish ; Petroff et al. 2015 (lead author; SUPERB survey searching for pulsars and FRBs)
- **Note:** 'Needed to know if they were just local noise too' paraphrases the paper's stated aim: to address whether perytons and FRBs could have a common origin.

### c7 · high confidence · beat `story`
> In January 2015 (the week of 19 January) three perytons were detected, and a newly installed RFI monitor (December 2014) showed each came with emission at 2.3–2.5 GHz, the band microwave ovens emit in.
- **Source:** Petroff et al. 2015, arXiv:1504.02165, sections 2–3 and Conclusions: 'In December 2014 an RFI monitoring system was installed on the Parkes site'; 'Three peryton detections… during the week of 19 January, 2015'; 'As microwave ovens are known to emit in this frequency range'
- **Note:** Spoken as 'a new radio monitor'. Microwave ovens operate at about 2.45 GHz.

### c8 · high confidence · beat `story`
> On 17 March 2015 the team ran the staff kitchen microwave and stopped it by opening the door before the cycle finished, producing perytons at the exact times the door was opened.
- **Source:** Petroff et al. 2015, arXiv:1504.02165, section 3: 'The decisive test occurred on 17 March, 2015… instead of waiting for the microwave cycle to finish the microwave was stopped by opening the door. This test produced 3 bright perytons from the staff kitchen microwave all at the exact times of opening the microwave oven door'
- **Note:** 'Right on cue' = at the exact time of the door opening; the paper notes about a 50% success rate overall.

### c9 · high confidence · beat `point`
> On-site microwaves could not have produced the first fast radio burst (FRB 010724), and FRBs remained strong candidates for genuine extragalactic signals.
- **Source:** Petroff et al. 2015, arXiv:1504.02165, abstract: 'microwaves on site could not have caused FRB 010724… FRBs are strong candidates for genuine extragalactic transients'

### c10 · high confidence · beat `point`

- **Source:** Chatterjee et al. 2017, 'A direct localization of a fast radio burst and its host', Nature 541, 58–61, doi:10.1038/nature20797

### c11 · high confidence · beat `kicker`

- **Source:** Petroff et al. 2015, arXiv:1504.02165 (perytons detected since 1998; source identified March 2015)
