# AGENTS.md — house rules

You operate a content engine for faceless short-video channels. The engine is generic: every channel is
data in `channels/<slug>/`, and the same pipeline makes videos for all of them. These rules apply to every
channel and every step. Channel files can add rules (tone, banned topics); they can never relax these.

## 1. Never mix channels

- A generation step loads **only**: this file + the target channel's files + the skills its series uses.
  The engine assembles exactly that into a task file (`…/tasks/<step>.md`). Read the task file — nothing else.
- Never copy characters, catchphrases, jokes, ideas, learnings or visual identity from one channel to another.
- Write only inside the target channel's folder (or its own `drafts/<slug>/` folder).

## 2. Nothing gets deleted

- Ideas are marked `used` or `rejected`, drafts `approved` or `rejected` — never removed.
- Channels are never overwritten; to change one, edit its files. The CLI commits every change to git;
  never rewrite or squash that history.

## 3. People decide

- Never approve anything on the person's behalf. Channels (`approve channel`), drafts (`approve draft`) and
  fact-checks (`approve factcheck`) are approved only after the person has said so; `--by` names them.
- The fact-check is a hard gate: nothing is voiced or rendered until the person approves every claim.
  The approval is bound to the exact words and numbers — change any of them and it must be approved again.

## 4. Quality bar

- The hook lands in the first 2 seconds: a first sentence of about five words, plus on-screen text.
- One idea per video. Every beat earns its place: no filler, no "in this video…", no begging for likes.
- Specific beats vague: real numbers, real parts, comparisons the audience can picture.
- Write for the ear: short sentences, contractions, words the audience already knows.
- Stay inside the series length and structure, the channel's tone and its audience's age.

## 5. Facts

- Every factual statement — spoken, on screen, in a label, or a number in a graphic — is listed as a claim
  with a real source you have checked (manufacturer, standards body, encyclopedia, peer-reviewed or
  government source).
- Never invent a source, a quote or a statistic. If you can't verify something, cut it.
- When sources disagree, use the conservative version ("about", "most") and say so in the claim's note.

## 6. Safety

- Respect each channel's banned topics, including indirect references.
- No medical, legal or financial advice. No dangerous instructions or stunts anyone could copy.
- Kids' channels: nothing scary, violent or gross; never ask kids for personal information; keep
  `made_for_kids` correct in channel.md.
- No real people (likeness, voice or name used as a character), no brand mascots, no copyrighted
  characters. Characters are original (`original: true`).
- No hate, harassment, sexual content or misinformation. When in doubt, leave it out and ask.

## 7. AI labeling

- Every video is AI-generated (script, voice and visuals). Its `publish.json` always carries `ai_disclosure`
  with `ai_generated: true` and `label_as_ai_generated: true`, plus per-platform notes. It gets labeled
  wherever a platform requires it.
- Never present a synthetic voice as a real person.

## 8. Money and the outside world

- Never use a paid API (voice, LLM, stock media, anything) unless the person chose it. The defaults — the
  Kokoro voice and the `agent` LLM provider — are free.
- Keys live in `.env` only. Never print, log or commit them.
- Nothing is posted or scheduled: those features don't exist yet. Never upload a video anywhere.

## 9. How to work here

- Use the CLI: `./engine --help`. Follow `workflows/new-channel.md`, `workflows/pipeline.md` and `workflows/qa.md`.
- After writing any channel file, run `./engine validate --channel <slug>` and fix every error.
- When a command prints **"Waiting for the agent"**, open the task file it names, do exactly that, then run the
  command it gives you.
- When it prints **"STOP — human approval needed"**, stop and ask the person. Don't work around it.
- What you learn about a channel goes in that channel's `learnings.md` (through the QA review), nowhere else.

# Workflow: new channel

**seed → draft channel, characters, series and 10 ideas → the person approves → status `active`**

A channel is a niche + an audience + one or more characters + one or more series + an idea backlog, stored as
plain files in `channels/<slug>/`. Creating one never touches another channel, and an existing channel is
never overwritten.

## Three ways in

| You have | Run |
|---|---|
| Answers you want to give yourself | `./engine new channel` (asks questions) |
| A one-line idea | `./engine new channel --seed "space facts for kids" [--slug space-facts]` |
| A brainstormed niche you liked | `./engine brainstorm niches` → `./engine new channel --from-draft drafts/niches/<file>.md` |

## Seed / niche path, step by step

1. **Engine** creates `channels/<slug>/` with `channel.md` (the annotated template; `status: draft`),
   `ideas.md`, `learnings.md`, and the agent task `channels/<slug>/tasks/draft-channel.md`. It commits.
2. **Agent** reads only that task file and writes, inside `channels/<slug>/`:
   - `channel.md` — every field, plus the Promise section.
   - `characters/<name>.md` + `characters/<name>/model.json` — an original character; the rig and
     parameters come from `skills/render-talking-character/SKILL.md`. Expressions: at least `neutral`,
     `curious`, `excited`.
   - `series/<slug>.md` — beats that add up to the length; `skills.render` only from existing render skills.
   - `ideas.md` — exactly 10 ideas, status `new`, each with a one-line angle.
3. **Agent** runs `./engine validate --channel <slug>` until it reports no errors, then summarises the
   channel for the person (niche, audience, character, series, the 10 ideas) and asks for approval.
4. **Person** reviews and edits whatever they like (`./engine edit channel --channel <slug>` etc.).
5. **Person** approves: `./engine approve channel <slug> --by "<name>"` → `status: active`. The command
   refuses while validation fails. Agents never run this on their own.
6. First episode: `./engine make episode --channel <slug> --series <series>` (see `workflows/pipeline.md`).

## What a good channel draft has

- A niche narrow enough to be recognisable, wide enough for 100+ episodes.
- An audience you can picture (age, what they already know, why they'd watch).
- A character with a clear look that a rig can draw, a voice that fits, flaws, and a way of talking.
- A series with a repeatable format and a hook pattern that works in 2 seconds.
- Banned topics that match the audience (stricter for kids).
- Ideas that are specific ("a ballpoint pen", not "office supplies").

## Changing a channel later

- Add: `./engine add character|series --channel <slug>`, `./engine add ideas --channel <slug> --series <s> "…"`
- Brainstorm for approval: `./engine brainstorm characters|series|ideas --channel <slug>`
- Edit any file directly (or `./engine edit …`), then `./engine validate --channel <slug>`.
- Pause: `./engine set-status channel <slug> paused`.
</file>

# Skill: brainstorm

Brainstorming never changes a channel. You write **drafts** (in `drafts/`) and a person approves or rejects each
one. Propose fewer, better options; make each one concrete enough to approve as is.

## Principles

- **Specific over generic.** "Inside It: a ballpoint pen", not "everyday objects". A niche you can describe in one
  sentence to a stranger.
- **Repeatable.** A series must work for 100+ episodes with the same structure. An idea must fit its series' beats.
- **Different.** Each option differs from the others and from what already exists (other niches; this channel's
  characters, series and ideas — including used and rejected ones).
- **Producible with the engine as it is.** Use only the render skills listed in your task; characters must be
  drawable by an existing rig (see render-talking-character); voices from the voice list.
- **Original and safe.** No existing IP, real people, brand mascots or trends that need copyrighted audio. Follow
  AGENTS.md safety rules; think about fact-checking load (how hard is it to verify every episode?).

## Niches (`./engine brainstorm niches`)

One file per proposal: `drafts/niches/<date>-<slug>.md`, filled from `templates/niche.md`. For each, give the
audience, why it works (demand, repeatability, what's different), the character, the first series (format,
length, beats), the visual style + render skills, three sample ideas, and honest risks. Spread the options:
different audiences, visual styles and characters.

## Characters (`./engine brainstorm characters --channel <slug>`)

`drafts/<channel>/characters/<name>.md` (templates/character.md) + `drafts/<channel>/characters/<name>/model.json`,
with `draft: {kind: character, channel: <slug>, status: proposed, created: <date>}` in the frontmatter. A good
character has one strong visual idea, a voice that fits, a clear want, and flaws (`bad_at`). It must suit the
channel's audience and tone — and must not clone the channel's existing characters.

## Series (`./engine brainstorm series --channel <slug>`)

`drafts/<channel>/series/<slug>.md` (templates/series.md) with the draft block. Give a format line, a hook
pattern that works in 2 seconds, 3–6 beats whose seconds add up to the length, the visual style, and render
skills chosen from the list. Use this channel's characters.

## Ideas (`./engine brainstorm ideas --channel <slug> [--series <s>]`)

One file `drafts/<channel>/ideas/<date>-<name>.md`: frontmatter with the draft block, then ideas in the ideas
format with `status: proposed`:

```markdown
## zipper
- title: Inside It: a zipper
- series: inside-it
- status: proposed
- angle: How do two rows of teeth lock so tight — and why does the slider open them so easily?
- added: 2026-01-31
```

A good idea has a question the audience already wonders about, a payoff that fits the series' length, and facts
that can be verified from solid sources.
</file>

# Skill: render-talking-character

An overlay skill: it draws the speaking character on top of whatever scene skill a beat uses. The mouth opens with
the loudness of the voice audio (a per-frame envelope from the voice step); eyes blink on their own; each beat sets a
placement and an expression.

## Per beat: `beats[].character`

```jsonc
{ "placement": "corner", "expression": "curious" }
```

- `placement`: `full` (large, lower half — the scene is framed above it; good for hooks and outros), `corner`
  (small, lower left, above the captions), `hidden`.
- `expression`: a key from the character's `model.json` → `expressions`.

## The character's look: `characters/<name>/model.json`

A character picks a **rig** (a generic, parameterised character builder) and sets its parameters. New characters
need no code — only a new model.json.

### Rig `orb-bot-3d` — a round 3D robot

```jsonc
{
  "rig": "orb-bot-3d",
  "params": {
    "body":    { "shape": "sphere", "color": "#c8ced8", "metalness": 1, "roughness": 0.2, "facets": 24 },  // sphere | capsule | rounded-box; facets < 20 = low-poly
    "eye":     { "count": 1, "size": 0.42, "sclera": "#f5f8fc", "iris": "#29d3ff", "pupil": "#0a0e14", "glow": 0.7 },
    "mouth":   { "style": "led-bar", "color": "#29d3ff", "width": 0.34 },       // led-bar | slit | none
    "antenna": { "enabled": true, "color": "#9aa3b0", "tip": "#ff5a3c", "length": 0.45 },
    "accent": "#ff5a3c",      // ear bolts
    "arms": "stubs",          // stubs | none
    "hover": 0.05             // idle float
  },
  "expressions": { "neutral": { … }, "curious": { … } }
}
```

### Rig `flat-2d` — a flat vector creature

```jsonc
{
  "rig": "flat-2d",
  "params": {
    "body":  { "shape": "blob", "color": "#ffd166", "shade": "#f4a261", "outline": "#1d1b26", "outline_width": 7 },  // circle | blob | rounded-rect | drop | star | egg
    "eyes":  { "style": "round", "white": "#ffffff", "pupil": "#1d1b26", "spacing": 0.36, "y": -0.12, "size": 0.17 },   // round | dot | oval | sleepy
    "mouth": { "color": "#3b1f2b", "tongue": "#ff7a8a", "width": 0.3, "y": 0.2 },
    "cheeks": "#ff8fab",
    "accessories": [ { "type": "tail", "color": "#7ad3ff", "accent": "#ffffff" } ]  // tail | ears | antenna | sparkles | glasses | leaf | hat | crown | bow
  },
  "expressions": { "neutral": { … } }
}
```

### Rig `stick-figure` — a round-headed doodle person

Drawn with sketchy hand-drawn lines. It is the character of the `render-storybook` skill, which poses it inside
each scene (see that skill); on other scene skills this layer shows it like any other rig.

```jsonc
{
  "rig": "stick-figure",
  "params": {
    "head": "#f1d9a6", "line": "#3a332d", "line_width": 5.5, "head_size": 1,   // head_size 0.8–1.4
    "hair": "tuft",            // tuft | none | curl | spiky | bun
    "hair_color": "#3a332d",
    "body_style": "stick",     // stick | tunic (a small filled body)
    "body_color": "#d9b97e",
    "accessory": "none",       // none | glasses | beanie | chef-hat | helmet | scarf | bow | cap
    "accessory_color": "#c2553a"
  },
  "expressions": { "neutral": { … } }
}
```

### Expressions (same fields for every rig; all optional)

| field | range | effect |
|---|---|---|
| `eye_scale` | 0.5–1.6 | bigger / smaller eyes |
| `pupil_scale` | 0.4–1.6 | tiny pupils = surprise |
| `lid` | 0–0.9 | half-closed eyes (sleepy, sceptical) |
| `tilt_deg` | -25–25 | head tilt |
| `look` | [-1..1, -1..1] | gaze direction |
| `bounce` | 0–1 | how much it bobs while talking |
| `brow` | flat \| up \| down \| worried | brows (2D and stick-figure rigs) |
| `mouth_curve` | -1–1 | frown … smile (2D and stick-figure rigs) |
| `glow` | 0–1.5 | eye/LED glow (robot rig) |

Every model needs at least `neutral`. Recommended set: `neutral`, `curious`, `excited`, `surprised`, `thinking`.
</file>

# Skill: render-storybook

A scene skill: during the beats that use it, it fills the frame with a square illustrated card (full width, from
y≈420 to y≈1500) and a title bar above it. You never draw pixels — you **describe scenes** from a fixed library of
settings, poses and props, and the skill draws them with wobbly hand-drawn lines, muted watercolour washes and paper
grain. Same script → same picture (fixed seeds), so re-renders are stable.

It suits stories told one picture at a time: a narrator character acting out a metaphor ("if you took one step
every…"), each sentence its own scene. Pair it with the `captions` skill and `caption_style: single-word` in the
series (one word at a time in the middle of the picture) and `skills.render: [render-storybook]` (no separate
character layer — the character lives inside the scenes).

## Title bar: `visuals["render-storybook"]`

```jsonc
{
  "title": "A billion seconds is how long?",  // 3–64 chars, shown on top for the whole video, upper-cased
  "highlight": ["billion seconds"]             // ≤ 4 parts of the title drawn in the channel's accent colour
}
```

Keep titles **≤ 30 characters** so they fit on one line at a readable size (longer titles wrap into two balanced
lines and get smaller). The title is on screen the whole time → its words and numbers are claims too.

## Each beat: `visual`

```jsonc
{
  "skill": "render-storybook",
  "scenes": [ /* 1–6 scenes, one per sentence of the beat's voiceover, in order */ ]
}
```

**Timing is automatic:** scene 1 starts with the beat, scene 2 with the beat's second sentence, and so on (a short
cross-fade between scenes). More scenes than sentences split the last sentence evenly. Fewer scenes than sentences
keep the last scene up. So write the voiceover in short sentences and give each one a picture.

### A scene

```jsonc
{
  "setting": "hills",        // hills | beach | sea | plain | desert | snow | night | space | room | city
  "time": "day",             // day | dusk | night   (night and space settings are always night)
  "camera": "push-in",       // still | push-in | pull-out | pan-left | pan-right   (slow move over the scene)
  "character": {             // the beat's speaker, posed in the scene; null = no character in this scene
    "pose": "point",         // stand | point | wave | think | shrug | cheer | hold | walk | sit | look-up | facepalm
    "x": 0.3,                // 0 = left edge … 1 = right edge (feet always on the ground)
    "scale": 1,              // 1 ≈ 37% of the picture's height; 0.3–2.5
    "facing": "right",       // left | right
    "expression": "curious", // a key from the character's model.json → expressions
    "hold": "clock",         // optional: cards | paper | book | clock | coins | drop | globe | sign | candle
    "hold_text": "52!"       // optional: ≤ 12 chars written on a held sign / paper / book
  },
  "others": [                // ≤ 4 extra figures (same look, they don't talk) — crowds, "you", a friend
    { "pose": "stand", "x": 0.62, "scale": 0.8, "facing": "left" }
  ],
  "props": [                 // ≤ 8 props (below)
    { "type": "sign", "x": 0.72, "text": "1,000,000,000" }
  ]
}
```

Settings: `hills` rolling sage hills · `plain` flat grassland · `beach` sand, sea and a headland · `sea` open water
with a strip of shore · `desert` dunes · `snow` white hills · `night` hills under stars · `space` stars only ·
`room` wall, floor, window · `city` skyline behind a street.

### Props

## Layout rules (read before placing anything)

- The picture is a 1000×1000 grid in scene units; the ground line is at about y = 830 (845–860 on beach, sea,
  room and city).
- **Caption band:** single-word captions appear at scene y ≈ 330–390, horizontally centred. Keep faces and
  lettering out of that band: standing characters at `scale` ≤ 1.2 (normal shots) or ≥ 1.9 (close-ups, the face is
  above the band) — scales in between put the caption across the face.
- Sky lettering (`text`, `speech`, `thought`) goes at y ≤ 0.28. Keep everything inside 0.05–0.95: the picture's
  edges are darkened by a vignette.
- One focal point per scene: the character plus 1–3 props. Leave air around the character.
- Every scene is a new picture: change the setting, the camera, the pose or the props from one sentence to the
  next. Escalate — the story's scenes should get bigger (one jar → a row of jars → the ocean).

## Writing for this skill

- The hook beat's first scene shows the number itself (`sign`, `text` or `calendar`) with the character reacting.
- Numbers on props are exactly the numbers in the voiceover ("1,000,000,000" ↔ "a billion"), rounded the same way.
- Every word on a prop (`text`, `hold_text`) and the title are on-screen text: factual ones need a claim with a
  source in `claims`, and the fact-check covers them.
- Short lettering: props ≤ 28 characters, held signs ≤ 12. Use digits with commas for big numbers.

## Checks the engine runs

`./engine make episode` rejects a script (and lists every problem) when: a scene uses an unknown setting/pose/prop,
an `arrow`/`footprints` has no `x2`, a `sign`/`calendar`/`speech`/`text` has no `text`, a beat has many more scenes
than sentences, or an `expression` isn't in the speaker's model.json.
