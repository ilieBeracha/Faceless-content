### c1 · high confidence · beat `hook`
> Oenology students given a white wine dyed red generally described it with the smell words they had used for a red wine; the authors say that, because of what they saw, the tasters discounted what they smelled.
- **Source:** https://web.stanford.edu/class/linguist62n/morrot01colorofodors.pdf
- **The source says:**
  - "Brain and Language 79, 309–320 (2001)"
  - "Published online August 28, 2001"
  - "Frédéric Brochet and Denis Dubourdieu"
  - "Our study first focuses on the influence of vision on smell by carrying out a lexical analysis of four wine experts’ tasting comments."
  - "The 7000 tasting comments were written by Robert Parker."
  - "The analysis shows that the odors of a wine are, for the most part, represented by objects that have the color of the wine."
  - "Evidence of the relationship between the perceived odor and the color of a wine initially led us to hypothesize that the color of a wine directs the identification of its odor."
  - "containing sémillon and sauvignon grapes for the white wine (wine W), and cabernet-sauvignon and merlot grapes for the red wine (wine R)."
  - "vintage 1996"
  - "Part of the white wine was colored red (wine RW) with 2 g/L of purified grape anthocyanins"
  - "The neutrality test of the dye was carried out by 50 people recruited on the ENSIA Massy campus."
  - "It is therefore concluded that wines W and RW are perceived as identical and that the dye can be regarded as having no odor."
  - "The wine comparison test was carried out by 54 undergraduates from the Faculty of Oenology of the University of Bordeaux."
  - "The wine comparison test was carried out in two sessions separated by 1 week."
  - "The wines were presented in clear AFNOR glasses, under artificial white lighting."
  - "During first session, the subjects were requested to draw up a list of odor descriptors for wine W and R."
  - "During the first session, the subjects chose odors represented by red or dark objects to describe wine R and yellow or clear objects to describe wine W."
  - "cassis (blackcurrant)"
  - "framboise (raspberry)"
  - "miel (honey)"
  - "litchi (lychee)"
  - "The two wines then proposed were W and RW."
  - "For the second session, each subject received his/her own list of descriptors, from the first session, given in alphabetical order and without indications concerning the wines."
  - "During the second session, the subjects generally used the odor descriptors they had used to characterize wine R to describe the wine RW."
  - "Moreover, in their olfactory description of wine RW, they eliminated the descriptors they had used for wine W."
  - "Hence, because of the visual information, the tasters discounted the olfactory information."
  - "The observed phenomenon is a real perceptual illusion."
- **Note:** Dossier f14, f25, f27. "Fooled" = the authors' finding that visual information made the tasters discount the olfactory information (f27), which they call a real perceptual illusion (f28). The students were not asked the wine's colour; no count of how many.
